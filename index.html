Hydration % — the science

Hydration % = water ÷ flour × 100 (flour is always 100%; this is a baker's percentage). It includes the water in your levain — e.g. 100 g of a 100%-hydration levain adds ~50 g water + 50 g flour to the totals.

Water (1) hydrates the gluten proteins (glutenin + gliadin) → more water = stretchier but weaker gluten; (2) hydrates starch → sets the crumb at bake; (3) feeds fermentation and creates steam in the oven → oven spring + crisper crust. More water also makes the dough more fluid, so gas pockets merge into bigger holes → an open, lacy crumb.

Higher hydration is not "better" — it's a trade-off. Lower = easier, tighter crumb. Higher = airier, harder.

The trade-off
Low (~60–68%)High (~75–85%+)
CrumbTight, even, softOpen, airy, big holes
HandlingEasy, clean, holds shapeWet, sticky, slack

What changes the "real" feel
  • Whole grain absorbs far more water → a 70% wholemeal dough handles like ~65% white. (This is why your 71% + 25% wholemeal felt so wet.)
  • Fat (oil/butter) & honey lubricate → softer/wetter at the same %.
  • Seeds / chia soak up water → can dry the dough out (pre-soak seeds).
  • Temperature — warm dough feels stickier/wetter.

For easier handling: drop to ~68% (≈ −10 to −15 g water), master that, then climb. Higher hydration is a reward for good technique.
